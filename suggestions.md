## Shape of every recipe

Every recipe is a complete meal built from exactly three parts:

- **One protein**, e.g. chicken, beef, eggs, tofu, fish, lentils.
- **One vegetable**, the main one the dish is built around.
- **One carb**, e.g. rice, noodles, bread, tortillas, potatoes.

For example: chicken, broccoli, rice.

Rules for picking the three:

- Take the vegetable from the TO USE list whenever the list has one, starting
  with the most urgent.
- If the TO USE list has a protein or a carb, use it. Otherwise choose a common
  one that suits the dish and list it under "needs".
- Rice is always on hand, so prefer it as the carb unless another carb clearly
  suits the dish better.
- Aromatics, herbs and chilies (ginger, garlic, cilantro, green chili,
  jalapeño and the like) are flavourings. Use them freely alongside the three
  parts, but they never count as the vegetable.
- Don't add a second protein, a second main vegetable or a second carb.
- Start the "method" with the three parts in the form
  "Protein: chicken. Veg: broccoli. Carb: rice." and then give the steps.

## Pantry staples

These are always in the kitchen. Use them freely and never list them under
"needs":

- Oil (neutral cooking oil and olive oil)
- Rice
- Salt and black pepper
- Garlic and onions
- Soy sauce
- Vinegar
- Sugar
- Butter
- Flour
- Dried spices: cumin, coriander, turmeric, paprika, chili powder, chili flakes

Anything else a recipe requires goes under "needs". Keep that list short:
ideally just the protein, and at most three items.

## How to choose

- Across the five recipes, cover as many of the TO USE ingredients as possible,
  and make sure the most urgent ones each appear in at least one recipe.
- Vary the five: different proteins and different cooking methods, not five
  versions of the same stir-fry.
- Favour recipes that use up a good amount of the listed ingredient, not a
  garnish-sized pinch.
